Enigma Lab is a simulation, not an animation. Every letter you type steps the rotors, travels through the plugboard, crosses the reflector and comes back through a different path to light a lamp. Change a ring setting and the ciphertext changes, because the wiring really did move.

Two interfaces, one machine

The form lists every setting an operator would read off a key sheet: rotor order, ring settings, window positions and plugboard cables. The 3D view is the same machine as an object you can turn over, lift the lid on and tap. They share one state, so a letter typed in one appears in the other, and the URL always describes the machine you are looking at.

What the simulation gets right

  • The wiring. Rotors I–VIII, the thin Beta and Gamma rotors, and reflectors UKW-A, B, C and the M4 thin pair, with the published measurements behind each.
  • The notches. Rotor I turns over at Q, rotor V at Z, the naval rotors VI–VIII at Z and M. Which is why the machine can double-step.
  • The double step. The middle rotor advances when the right-hand rotor reaches its notch, and again when it reaches its own. Operators did not want it; the machine did it anyway.
  • The procedure. A message key was sent twice, encrypted with the daily key. That repetition is the leak the Polish Cipher Bureau and Bletchley Park attacked, and the signal panel shows it happening.

What it does not pretend

This is not a reconstruction of a specific serial number. The plugboard, the lampboard and the case are representations, not measurements. Ciphertext is not a claim about any real message, and nothing here is encryption you should rely on — Enigma was broken, and this is a teaching machine.

Who actually broke it

The machine was never defeated by arithmetic. In 1932 the Polish Cipher Bureau handed the problem to three young mathematicians from Poznań — Marian Rejewski, Jerzy Różycki and Henryk Zygalski — and Rejewski reconstructed the military wiring from intercepted indicators before the end of that year. Różycki's clock method and Zygalski's perforated sheets carried the attack forward. In July 1939, with war days away, the Poles handed everything they had to British and French intelligence at a meeting outside Warsaw.

At Bletchley Park, Alan Turing and Gordon Welchman turned that inheritance into the bombe, with Harold "Doc" Keen's engineers at BTM building it. Hut 6 attacked army and air-force traffic; Hut 8 took the naval problem, which the four-rotor M4 made far harder.

Hut 6 Ultra would never have got off the ground if we had not learned from the Poles, in the nick of time, the details both of the German military version of the commercial Enigma machine, and of the operating procedures that were in use. — Gordon Welchman

How it is built

The mathematics lives in a separate, tested library: @enigma-lab/core, which is headless, has no runtime dependencies and ships as ESM, CommonJS and UMD. This site pins one release of it by sha256 and refuses to build against anything else. Rendering splits by job: WebGPU draws the top-down rotor discs where a device offers an adapter, SVG draws the letters and controls, and the 3D view is a small painter's-algorithm renderer on a 2D canvas so it works on every device.

There is no backend, no account and no analytics. The machine is stored in the URL; your preferences stay in localStorage on this device.
